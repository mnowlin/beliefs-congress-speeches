## 3. Information sources (RQ2)

- **Sources are common.** 81% of speeches cite at least one source (C&T 80%, IRA 83%; D 82%, R 81%). Mean 3.2 per speech, median 2. One speech (Inhofe, 2009-01-08, 7,780 words, a catalogue of dissenting scientists and media reports) has 59; without it the mean is 2.8. **Analyze presence per speech × type, not raw counts.**
- **75% of sources are named** (`specific = 1`): D 73%, R 78%. Generic appeals are concentrated in `constituent` (12% named) and `other` (44%).
- **`use`:** 435 support, 31 dispute, 13 mixed. Disputed sources are mostly `executive`, `media`, and `international` (≈20% each).
- **`evidence_form`:** finding 188, position 116, quantitative 112, testimony 63. Democrats lean slightly more on numbers (26% vs. 21%).
- **21% of sources (102) are linked to no concept**, mostly endorsements and bill-stance support.
- **12 inserted documents** were recorded as sources.

**Early RQ2/RQ3 signals** (150 speeches, one LLM coder; descriptive only):
- Science citation is partisan and falls across eras: D 47% vs. R 22%; C&T 44% → IRA 27%. This matches the keyword scan of the full corpus.
- Republicans cite media (17% vs. 6%) and constituents (18% vs. 8%) more; Democrats cite advocacy groups more (15% vs. 4%).
- International sources nearly vanish in the IRA era (15% → 3%); constituent testimony rises (9% → 16%).
- Science sources are used on both sides: Inhofe's dissenting scientists are coded `science`, `use = support`. `use` records the speaker's stance toward the source, not the source's stance toward the consensus, so "contrarian science" is not identifiable from the current fields.

## 4. Hedging (Section 4a)

- `hedged` = 1 on **3.2%** of statements (v0.4 `speaker_certainty` = 1: 3.9%). It reproduces the baseline as intended.
- **The partisan gap on climate science holds:** PC1–PC3 statements hedged, R **12.9%** (n = 31) vs. D **0.9%** (n = 116). In v0.4: R 12.1%, D 3.6%. The Republican count is small.
- **The dictionary is a weak check.** Of 43 statements whose quote contains a dictionary hedge, the coder marked 15 hedged; most of the other 28 are predictions ("prices could rise"), which §4a says are not hedges. The speech-level correlation between the share of hedged statements and dictionary hedges per 1,000 words is **0.14**. Dictionary rates: R slightly higher on both hedges (3.4 vs. 3.0) and boosters (1.4 vs. 1.0); C&T speeches hedge more than IRA speeches (3.9 vs. 2.4).

## 5. Concepts and stability

- Statements per codable speech: 5.33 (v0.4: 5.17). 30 of 31 concepts meet the 5% criterion in at least one corpus; **SC13 fails again** (2.0%). PC11 (IRA 5.3%), PC12 (C&T 5.3%), and SC2 (C&T 6.7%) pass only barely.
- **Re-code stability vs. v0.4** (identical concept definitions; v0.5 coders were independent subagents that never saw v0.4 codes): median κ **0.88** presence, **0.89** signed. Lowest: **DC4 0.69, DC1 0.72, PC4 0.74, SC4 0.76**. In v0.4 the lowest was PC6 (0.64); PC6 is 0.82 here. These are same-model agreements and overstate human reliability.

## 6. Recommended changes for the final v0.5 (before human coding)

**Decisions (2026-10-23):** items 1 and 3 adopted as written; item 2 resolved by adding a new `expert_generic` type (with a rule that generic appeals about the physical climate stay `science`); item 4 recorded as a note and open issue, with no new field; item 6 done (boundary anchors for DC1, DC4, PC4, and SC4 in codebook §4). The final codebook has 17 source types; the final prompt is `codebook/prompt_v0.5.txt` (md5 `99c9df718d03ba342379e03b7900db4e`). The codes in this report were made with the draft prompt and use the draft's 15 types.

1. **Split `other` into three new types** that the pilot surfaced:
   - `courts`: Supreme Court and district court rulings (≈9 sources in this pilot).
   - `public_opinion`: polls and survey results (≈8).
   - `congress`: committee reports, hearing records, and CRS-style congressional products, as distinct from Members' floor remarks, which stay excluded (≈5).
2. **Add a rule for unattributed estimates.** "Estimates of cost," "economists say," "some estimates" (≈10 in `other`) should be coded with the type of expert they invoke where clear (economists → `govt_analysis` if official, otherwise `think_tank`), else `other` with `specific = 0`. Alternatively, create `expert_generic`. **This is your call.**
3. **Merge `labor` into `advocacy` and `religious` into `other`.** Both fail the 5% criterion. Keep `military_security`, `think_tank`, and `international`, which pass in one corpus.
4. **Optional: add a source field for contrarian science** (`consensus`: mainstream / dissenting / not applicable) if RQ2 needs to separate consensus science from dissenting scientists. Without it, Republican "science" citations mix both.
5. **Keep `hedged`; demote the dictionary.** Report dictionary rates as a separate descriptive measure of style, not as validation of `hedged`.
6. **Flag DC4, DC1, PC4, and SC4** as the concepts most likely to fail human reliability; add anchor examples for each before the human study.
7. **Analysis rule:** measure source salience as presence per speech (or per speech × type), not counts, so that catalogue speeches do not dominate.
